## Caption

Parents often want respect to increase.
But rarely pause to understand where it’s coming from.

From childhood, children are taught just one rule:
“Don’t talk back to elders.”
Even when the elder is wrong.
Even when the child is hurt.
Even when silence costs self-respect.

So the child bows their head.
Swallows their words.
And learns that obedience is safer than honesty.

That is not respect.
That is fear, dressed up as culture.

When a child finally speaks,
it’s called attitude.
Not courage.
Not self-expression.

But real respect is never forced.
It grows when there is listening.
When there is understanding.
When there is space on both sides.

Hierarchy can demand silence.
But respect requires connection.

Silence is not culture.
Understanding is.
