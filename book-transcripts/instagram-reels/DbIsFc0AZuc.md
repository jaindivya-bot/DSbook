## Caption

Most purchases don’t stay isolated.

One upgrade quietly creates
the desire for another.

A new phone makes the old accessories
feel outdated.

A new car makes other parts of life
suddenly seem less impressive.

And before you realise it,
you are spending money
not because you need more—
but because your standards changed.

This is how lifestyle inflation begins.

The danger isn’t buying nice things.

The danger is letting every upgrade
create a new level of dissatisfaction.

Because if happiness depends
on constantly replacing what you already have,
contentment becomes impossible.

Enjoy improvement.
But don’t let possessions decide
whether your life feels complete.

Sometimes the most valuable skill
is knowing when enough is enough.
