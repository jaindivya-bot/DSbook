## Caption

During a conversation with Divya Jain (@thedivyajainpodcast), finance educator Neha Nagar(@iamnehanagar), highlighted an aspect of personal finance that often gets overlooked—learning how to spend money wisely. While conversations around saving and investing have become more common, she argued that very few people are taught how to make thoughtful spending decisions.

👉🏼 The Missing Money Lesson

According to Neha, nearly 60–70% of a person's income is typically spent on everyday expenses, yet many people don't actively track where their money goes. She noted that digital payments and quick-commerce platforms have made spending more convenient, but they've also made it easier to lose track of everyday expenses.

👉🏼 Spend With Intention

Rather than focusing only on saving every rupee, Neha encouraged people to become more aware of their spending habits. She argued that tracking expenses and making conscious financial decisions can often improve long-term financial health without requiring major lifestyle sacrifices.

👉🏼 The Logical Indian's Perspective

Financial literacy goes beyond earning, saving, and investing. Developing mindful spending habits and understanding where money goes can be just as important for building long-term financial well-being.
